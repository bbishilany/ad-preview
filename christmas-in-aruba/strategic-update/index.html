The campaign is doing its job.

Christmas in Aruba · Campaign launch (July 28) through August 24, 2026 · Prepared September 1

Four weeks in, the campaign finds buyers at about $4 per add-to-cart in a market of roughly 106,000 people, and it converted paid traffic into pre-orders from week one. Cost per result has crept up as the campaign saturates the small audience — the auction price of reaching people rose 73% in the latest period — and that is the expected pattern in a market this size, arriving on the schedule we predicted at launch. The lever that resets it is fresh creative, and that is our top recommendation below.

Orders & revenue

In the first 8 days, the campaign produced 5 Shopify pre-orders totaling AWG 2,064 (~$1,155) — roughly 6x return on the ad spend in that window, with cart-to-order running near 10%. Later reports tracked cart activity only, so treat that order count as a week-one floor; we are reconciling the full 28-day order picture in Shopify and will bring it to the next update. Pre-orders are partially paid by design — final collections land closer to delivery.

What we're seeing

1

The engine works

Volume has held near 5.7 add-to-carts per day for four straight weeks, with click-through at 5.7% early and 4.7% in the latest period. People in Aruba are responding to the product.

2

Costs are rising for a knowable reason

The increase is almost entirely auction price (CPM +73%), while the site itself is converting better — page-to-cart improved from 2.98% to 3.37%. The ads and the store are fine; the audience pool is tightening after 28 days of the same six creatives. This is the standard lifecycle of a campaign in a small market.

3

The ad set has a clear hierarchy

Meta rotated its favorite from “Preorders Close Soon” (71% of early budget) to “Christmas Comes Home” (56% of the latest period at $3.93 per cart, rated Above Average on conversion rate). “Reserve Now Save” has quietly been the cheapest converter in the account — $2.68 per cart — while receiving almost no budget. “Lit in Minutes” has effectively not converted.

4

Frequency ran ahead of schedule

The average person reached had seen the ads about 5 times by day 17. Our launch plan called for refreshing creative when frequency approaches 6 — the rising CPM in the latest period is that threshold announcing itself in dollars.

Recommendations

1 · Refresh the creative set nowHighest leverage

Three or more new variations, built around what the data endorsed: the homecoming/family angle of “Christmas Comes Home” and the savings-forward angle of “Reserve Now Save.” Retire “Lit in Minutes.”

2 · Give the savings message a fair test

“Reserve Now Save” earned carts at roughly 40% below account average and the algorithm underfed it. The refresh carries its offer framing into multiple new variants so budget can find it.

3 · Reconcile orders before the next spend decision

The week-one signal was strong; a 28-day Shopify pull tells us the true cart-to-order rate at scale and whether follow-up on partially-paid pre-orders is needed.

4 · Plan the seasonal budget step now

September and October are the natural pre-order window. At today’s ~$4 cost per cart and the current ~$750/month pace, the campaign has room to buy more volume at an efficiency we like. Once fresh creative stabilizes costs, we recommend agreeing the step-up level in advance so the scale decision is already made when the season arrives.

5 · Move ad-account billing back to your card

Interim billing has been running through Mileage since the payment interruption in early August. A 5-minute swap in Business Manager puts it back where it belongs before spend scales.
